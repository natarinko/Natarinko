# Data

## Introduction

**Data** are recorded observations, measurements, facts, symbols, or values that represent characteristics of objects, events, or phenomena. Data serve as the raw material from which information is produced and knowledge is developed through interpretation, analysis, and application.

Data are fundamental to science, mathematics, computing, engineering, economics, medicine, and virtually every other field of human activity. Modern societies generate enormous quantities of data every day through scientific research, business operations, communication networks, sensors, financial transactions, and digital devices.

The rapid growth of digital technology has transformed data into one of the world's most valuable resources. Organizations use data to improve decision-making, discover patterns, predict future events, and support innovation.

---

## Definition

Data are individual facts, measurements, observations, or symbols that have been collected for reference or analysis.

Unlike information, data do not necessarily possess meaning on their own. Their significance depends on interpretation and context.

For example:

- 18.4
- 2026
- Blue
- 37°C
- 10110101

Each of these values is data. Only when placed into context do they become meaningful information.

---

## Etymology

The word **data** comes from the Latin word *datum*, meaning "something given." In modern English, *data* is commonly treated as a collective noun referring to multiple pieces of recorded information, although some style guides also accept its use as a singular mass noun.

## Data, Information, and Knowledge

The relationship between data, information, and knowledge is often described as a hierarchy.

### Data

Raw observations or measurements.

Example:

```
22
24
27
30
29
```

### Information

Data organized into meaningful context.

Example:

> Daily temperatures increased throughout the week before declining slightly on the final day.

### Knowledge

Understanding gained by interpreting information together with experience and reasoning.

Example:

A meteorologist recognizes the temperature pattern as evidence of an approaching weather front.

---

## Characteristics of Data

Data generally possess several important characteristics.

### Accuracy

Data should correctly represent the observed phenomenon.

### Completeness

Missing data may reduce reliability and limit analysis.

### Consistency

Equivalent observations should be recorded using consistent formats and standards.

### Timeliness

Recently collected data are often more useful than outdated observations.

### Reliability

Reliable data are obtained through trustworthy methods and can be independently verified.

### Precision

Precision refers to the level of detail or exactness with which data are recorded.## Types of Data

Data may be classified in several ways depending on their nature, structure, and method of collection.

### Qualitative Data

Qualitative data describe characteristics or properties that cannot easily be measured numerically.

Examples include:

- colors;
- languages;
- opinions;
- species names;
- written descriptions.

Qualitative data are commonly used in the social sciences, medicine, and humanities.

---

### Quantitative Data

Quantitative data consist of numerical values that can be measured or counted.

Examples include:

- height;
- weight;
- temperature;
- population;
- income.

Quantitative data form the basis of mathematical, statistical, and scientific analysis.

---

### Structured Data

Structured data follow a predefined format and are usually organized in tables or databases.

Examples include:

- spreadsheets;
- SQL databases;
- census records;
- financial tables.

Structured data are easy for computers to search and process.

---

### Unstructured Data

Unstructured data have no fixed format.

Examples include:

- documents;
- photographs;
- videos;
- emails;
- audio recordings.

Most digital data created today are unstructured.

---

### Primary and Secondary Data

**Primary data** are collected directly by researchers through observation, experimentation, surveys, or measurement.

**Secondary data** are obtained from existing publications, databases, reports, or previous studies.

Both forms are valuable, although primary data generally provide greater control over quality and methodology.

---

## Data Collection

Data may be collected using many different methods.

Common approaches include:

- direct observation;
- scientific experiments;
- surveys and questionnaires;
- interviews;
- sensors and measuring instruments;
- satellite observations;
- computer systems;
- automated monitoring devices.

The choice of collection method depends on the objectives of the investigation and the nature of the phenomenon being studied.

---

## Data Processing

Raw data often require processing before they become useful.

Typical stages include:

1. Collection
2. Validation
3. Cleaning
4. Organization
5. Analysis
6. Visualization
7. Interpretation

Modern computers perform these operations rapidly, enabling organizations to analyze enormous datasets efficiently.

---

## Big Data

The term **Big Data** describes datasets whose size, complexity, or speed exceed the capabilities of traditional data-processing methods.

Big Data is often characterized by the "Five Vs":

- Volume
- Velocity
- Variety
- Veracity
- Value

Applications include:

- weather forecasting;
- medical research;
- financial analysis;
- transportation systems;
- artificial intelligence;
- environmental monitoring.

The growth of Big Data has significantly influenced modern science, engineering, and business.## Data Quality

The usefulness of data depends largely on its quality. High-quality data improve analysis, support informed decision-making, and increase confidence in research findings.

Important dimensions of data quality include:

- **Accuracy** — data correctly represent the observed phenomenon.
- **Completeness** — all necessary values are present.
- **Consistency** — data follow common standards and formats.
- **Validity** — data conform to the rules and definitions of the system in which they are used.
- **Timeliness** — data are sufficiently current for their intended purpose.
- **Uniqueness** — duplicate records are minimized or eliminated.

Poor-quality data may lead to incorrect conclusions, inefficient processes, or unreliable scientific results.

---

## Data Ethics

The collection and use of data raise important ethical considerations.

Key principles include:

- respecting individual privacy;
- obtaining informed consent when collecting personal data;
- ensuring fairness in data analysis;
- preventing discrimination;
- protecting confidential information;
- maintaining transparency in data use.

Researchers and organizations should follow applicable laws, ethical guidelines, and professional standards when handling data.

---

## Data Security

As digital data have become increasingly valuable, protecting them has become a major concern.

Common security measures include:

- encryption;
- authentication;
- access control;
- regular backups;
- network security;
- monitoring for unauthorized access.

Effective data security helps protect individuals, organizations, and governments from data loss, theft, and cyberattacks.

---

## Data in Scientific Research

Data are fundamental to the scientific method.

Scientists collect data through observation, experimentation, measurement, and simulation. These data are analyzed to identify patterns, test hypotheses, evaluate theories, and communicate results.

Modern scientific research increasingly emphasizes:

- reproducibility;
- transparency;
- data sharing where appropriate;
- proper documentation;
- long-term preservation of research data.

Well-managed datasets allow other researchers to verify findings and build upon previous work.

They are essential for:

- scientific research;
